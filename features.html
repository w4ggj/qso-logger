Logging

  • Nothing to install. Open the page and log. It keeps working with no internet, and installs to a phone or tablet home screen like an app.
  • Your log stays yours. It lives in your browser, not on somebody's server. No sign-up, no subscription, nothing to cancel.
  • Sessions — name a log per activation, contest or day, and switch between them.
  • Award programs built in — POTA, WWFF, SOTA, IOTA, BOTA, GMA, LOTA and WCA, as activator or hunter, exported to ADIF with the right tags.
  • GOTA operator tracking. Log who was actually on the mic. Its own column, a Students count, and it survives into the export.
  • Edit anything. Load a logged contact back into the form, fix it, save. A half-typed next QSO is parked and handed back.
  • ADIF and CSV export, plus backup and restore as a file.
  • Merge logs across devices. Log on the phone in the field, merge into the laptop at home. Duplicates are matched on content, not on row numbers, so re-importing the same file changes nothing.
  • Callsign lookups — QRZ, falling back automatically to free services. Fills in name, grid and QTH.
  • Offline, properly. Download the whole US callsign database to the device and keep looking up calls with no signal at all. It updates when you press the button, not behind your back.

Rig control

  • Live CAT over the USB cable. Frequency, mode and band read straight from the radio — no extra software, no shack-PC bridge. Desktop and Android.
  • Nineteen radios across three protocol drivers: Icom CI-V, Yaesu CAT, Kenwood and Elecraft. Full serial control per radio — baud, data bits, stop bits, parity and handshake.
  • The frequency field is the VFO knob. Click it and scroll to tune the radio: 1 kHz a step, Shift for 100 Hz, Alt for 10 kHz.
  • Band quick-jump. One tap per band, and each band remembers where you were — hop away and back and you return to your own frequency, not a canned one.
  • Set the mode from the logger — LSB, USB, LSB-D, USB-D, CW, AM, FM, RTTY, with the radio's current mode lit. On an Icom the filter you were using survives the change, which it does not if you ask carelessly.
  • PTT from the logger. Key the radio over the CAT cable with a button or a key you assign — a USB footpedal mapped to a function key becomes your PTT. Hold-to-talk or toggle, a transmit time limit, and Escape always unkeys.
What PTT actually sends, on an Icom
FE FE 94 E0 1C 00 01 FD   transmit
FE FE 94 E0 1C 00 00 FD   receive

Licence privileges

  • Pick your US licence class and the frequency box turns red when the dial leaves what that class may use for the mode you are logging.
  • It checks your sidebands, not just the dial. An SSB signal is about 3 kHz wide and sits to one side of the carrier, so a dial legally inside a segment can still put energy outside it. Sitting on 3.8005 LSB is inside General's phone segment and still wrong. The logger says so.
  • Off unless you turn it on, so operators outside the US are never shown a warning written for somebody else's rules.

Getting contacts home

  • Send each QSO to your shack as you log it — straight into the station software you already run, in the format it already understands, instead of exporting an ADIF and importing it by hand.
  • Live rig telemetry. Frequency, mode and transmit state sent home as you operate, so a shack dashboard follows your dial on phone the way it already does on digital.
  • Nothing is lost when the link drops. A contact that cannot be sent waits in a queue that survives a refresh or a closed laptop, and goes out when the connection returns. Logging never waits on the network.

Built for the field

  • Number pads on number fields, so a phone does not throw a full keyboard at you for a signal report.
  • A mobile layout that puts settings where you would look — at the foot of the page, not buried halfway up it.
  • An optional device PIN, to stop a shared shack laptop or a club tablet being opened by accident.

What you need

  • Rig control needs Chrome or Edge. The browser API it uses does not exist in Safari or Firefox, so there is no CAT control on an iPhone or iPad. Logging itself works in any browser.
  • Three radios are confirmed on real hardware — IC-706MKIIG, IC-7300 and FT-991A. The other sixteen are built from published protocols and are labelled untested in the picker until somebody reports back.
  • Sending contacts to the shack needs a small helper running on that computer, because a browser cannot send UDP. One file and a double-click — but it is not nothing.
